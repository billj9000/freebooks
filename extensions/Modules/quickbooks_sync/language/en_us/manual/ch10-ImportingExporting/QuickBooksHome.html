QuickBooks Sync

QuickBooks Sync copies your sales invoices and credit memos into QuickBooks Online, so that QuickBooks holds the same sales as FreeBooks. It is part of the optional QuickBooks Sync module, installed from Company → Configuration → Modules. It works with a QuickBooks company that charges VAT, such as a UK company; it does not handle US sales tax.

Before anything is sent, tell the module how your FreeBooks accounts, tax rates and customers correspond to QuickBooks': see QuickBooks Sync Settings.

Items and accounts

Every line of a QuickBooks invoice names a product or service, and that item decides which income account the line posts to. So each FreeBooks sales account is mapped to a QuickBooks item, set up in QuickBooks to post to the matching income account: for example a service called Sales for your sales account and another called Shipping for freight. Each line's SKU and description are written into the line's description.

How VAT and totals are kept equal

Each document states its own VAT: for every VAT code on it, the amount taxed and the VAT charged, which QuickBooks records in place of working the VAT out again. FreeBooks takes a discount off every product line in the same proportion before working out the VAT, and the document is built the same way, so its VAT matches what FreeBooks saved. FreeBooks rounds its VAT once for the whole document where QuickBooks keeps one figure per code; any penny between the two is taken into the largest VAT line.

If the document's total still differs from FreeBooks', a Rounding Adjustment line, sold as the rounding item, makes up the difference. A real line's amount is never changed.

FreeBooks stores every amount in your home currency. A document in another currency is converted at the document's own exchange rate before it is sent, so QuickBooks receives the amounts the customer was charged.
