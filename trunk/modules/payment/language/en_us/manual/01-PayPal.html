Payment Methods - PayPal

The PayPal payment method lets a customer pay an invoice through PayPal, by card or digital wallet, without needing a PayPal account of their own. From the Customer Receipt page you can:

  • create a payment link and copy it or email it to the customer;
  • create a PayPal invoice and have PayPal send it to the customer;
  • record a payment the customer has already made, by its PayPal transaction ID.

When the customer pays a link or a PayPal invoice, PayPal tells FreeBooks and the receipt is recorded automatically. A Customer Refund for an invoice paid through PayPal is sent back to PayPal against the original payment.

What you need

  1. A PayPal business account.
  2. A REST API app in the PayPal developer dashboard, which gives you a Client ID and a Client Secret. PayPal keeps separate apps for its Sandbox (test) environment and for live payments.
  3. For automatic receipts, FreeBooks must be reachable from the internet over HTTPS, so that PayPal can call it.

Installing and settings

Install the method from Company → Configuration → Payment Module (see Payment Methods), then open its settings:

  • PayPal REST API Client ID and Client Secret — from your PayPal REST API app.
  • Environment — Test uses PayPal's Sandbox, where no real money moves; Production takes real payments. The Client ID and Secret must belong to the same environment.
  • Email address to notify — an address to tell when a PayPal payment is recorded automatically, or when one needs a person to look at it. Leave it blank for no email.
  • Webhook ID — from PayPal, once you have registered the notification URL below. Automatic receipts need it.
  • Shared secret for the webhook — optional; see below.

Automatic receipts: registering the notification URL

In the PayPal developer dashboard, add a webhook to your app with this URL, using your own host name. NAME is the company's folder name under my_files/ on the server, which is also its database name — not the title shown on the login screen.

https://your-freebooks-host/paypal_webhook.php?company=NAME

If this installation has only one company, ?company=NAME may be left out. With more than one it is required, and a call without it is refused, so that a payment can never be recorded in the wrong company's books.

Subscribe the webhook to these two events:

  • Payment capture completed (PAYMENT.CAPTURE.COMPLETED) — a payment link was paid;
  • Invoicing invoice paid (INVOICING.INVOICE.PAID) — a PayPal invoice was paid.

Copy the Webhook ID PayPal shows into the method's settings and save.

Test mode and the webhook secret

In Production, FreeBooks asks PayPal to confirm that every notification really came from PayPal before acting on it. In Test it does not, so that PayPal's webhook simulator can be used — which means that in Test mode anyone who can reach the URL could send a notification. Set the webhook's Shared secret to close that: choose a long random value, enter it in the settings, and add it to the URL you register with PayPal:

https://your-freebooks-host/paypal_webhook.php?company=NAME&whsecret=YOUR-SECRET

Once a secret is set, every notification without it is refused, in Test and Production alike. Do not leave a company that takes real payments in Test mode.

Taking a payment

  1. Go to Banking → Customer Receipt and choose the customer.
  2. Tick the invoice to be paid.
  3. Choose PayPal as the payment method. Check the customer's email address.
  4. Either:
    • click Create Payment Link, then Copy it or click Email to Customer. A payment link expires after three hours; or
    • click Create PayPal Invoice, then Send Invoice to have PayPal email it to the customer.
  5. Close the page without saving the receipt. When the customer pays, FreeBooks records the receipt itself, carrying PayPal's transaction ID.

The payment is charged in the invoice's own currency.

One invoice per link. An automatic receipt is recorded against a single invoice. If you tick several invoices before creating a link or PayPal invoice, the payment is recorded against the first of them, up to what it owes, and the rest is reported to you (below) to record by hand.

Recording a payment by hand

To record a PayPal payment that has already been made, choose PayPal on the Customer Receipt, enter the payment's PayPal Transaction ID and save. FreeBooks checks with PayPal that the payment was completed before saving it, and keeps the ID so that a later refund can find the payment.

A receipt can also be saved with the Transaction ID left blank, as a payment you expect but cannot yet confirm. If the customer then pays through a link or PayPal invoice, the automatic receipt allows for it: see the next section. Once you have the transaction ID, open the saved receipt, enter it and save again, so that a refund can find the payment.

What an automatic receipt records

  • A Customer Receipt dated the day PayPal reports the payment, against the invoice, paid into the account set as the default for customer receipts (Company → Configuration → PhreeBooks Module, Customers tab). Its reference is PayPal's transaction ID.
  • The amount in the invoice's currency, at the invoice's own exchange rate, so a payment of the whole invoice closes it exactly.
  • Never more than the invoice still owes. If another receipt has already paid the invoice in whole or in part — for example one saved by hand with no Transaction ID — only the balance still due is recorded. Whatever PayPal took beyond it is left out, noted in the log, and, if a notification email is set, reported: the email gives the amount paid, the amount recorded and the amount not recorded. Check the other receipt. If it was for this same payment, enter the transaction ID on it; if it was a separate payment, the customer has paid twice and the difference is due back to them.
  • If PayPal reports a payment in a different currency from the invoice's, nothing is recorded and the log says so.

If a receipt cannot be recorded — for example because the payment date falls outside your accounting periods — nothing is kept, and FreeBooks tells PayPal the notification failed. PayPal sends it again later, so once the problem is fixed the receipt is recorded without anything further from you. A notification PayPal repeats is never recorded twice.

Refunds

Raise a Customer Credit Memo for the invoice, then a Customer Refund from the credit memo. When the invoice was paid through PayPal, the refund is sent back to PayPal against the original payment. This needs the payment's transaction ID on the receipt, which an automatic receipt always has.
