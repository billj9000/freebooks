Chart of Accounts

FreeBooks ships with eight example charts of accounts. The installer asks which one to use, and the same question is asked again when a further company is created from Company > Configuration > Company Manager:

  • UK - Sole Trader and Partnership (VAT)
  • UK - Limited Company (VAT) — preselected
  • Ireland - Sole Trader and Partnership (VAT)
  • Ireland - Limited Company (VAT)
  • US - General store (LLC and Partnerships)
  • US - General store (Corporations, S Corporations)
  • US - Retail store (LLC and Partnerships)
  • US - Retail store (Corporations, S Corporations)

The choice does two things beyond seeding the accounts themselves. It sets the default accounts the receivables and payables pages post to — each chart names its own, so a chart numbered differently does not leave those settings pointing at accounts that are not there. And where the chart names a currency, it sets the currency the company keeps its books in: the UK charts ask for sterling, the Irish ones for euro, the US ones for dollars.

Demonstration data. The sample customers, suppliers and stock that the installer can load name US account numbers directly, so they can only be loaded against the two US Retail charts. Choosing any other chart turns the demonstration data off; the installer says so if it had been asked for. Since a UK chart is preselected, an install left at its defaults is a clean company with no sample data — pick a US Retail chart to try FreeBooks with data already in it.

Changing the chart afterwards. A different chart can be imported later from Company > Configuration > FreeBooks > Chart of Accounts, either from the shipped list or from an XML file of your own. Ticking Replace current chart of accounts discards the existing chart and takes the new chart's default accounts with it — that is only possible while the general journal is still empty. Left unticked, the accounts are merged into the chart already in place and the existing defaults are left alone.

Each chart is an XML file under modules/phreebooks/language/en_us/charts/. An account entry looks like this:

<account>
  <id>1100</id>
  <description>Accounts Receivable</description>
  <heading>0</heading>
  <primary></primary>
  <type>2</type>
</account>

Ahead of the accounts, a chart may describe itself. All four entries are optional; a chart without them is read exactly as charts were before they existed:

<description>UK - Limited Company Chart of Accounts (VAT)</description>
<country>GB</country>
<currency>GBP</currency>
<demo>0</demo>
<defaults>
  <AR_DEFAULT_GL_ACCT>1100</AR_DEFAULT_GL_ACCT>
  <AR_DEF_GL_SALES_ACCT>4000</AR_DEF_GL_SALES_ACCT>
  ...
</defaults>

<description> is what the drop down shows. <currency> is the currency the books are kept in. <demo>0</demo> withholds the demonstration data, which is right for any chart not numbered as the US Retail charts are. <defaults> names which of the chart's own accounts each receivables and payables setting means; only settings FreeBooks already has are taken, so a misspelt entry is ignored rather than creating a setting of its own.

The Account types (the <type> value) are:

0 - Cash
2 - Accounts Receivable
4 - Inventory
6 - Other Current Assets
8 - Fixed Assets
10 - Accumulated Depreciation
12 - Other Assets
20 - Accounts Payable
22 - Other Current Liabilities
24 - Long Term Liabilities
30 - Income
32 - Cost of Sales
34 - Expenses
40 - Equity - Doesn't Close
42 - Equity - Gets Closed
44 - Equity - Retained Earnings
