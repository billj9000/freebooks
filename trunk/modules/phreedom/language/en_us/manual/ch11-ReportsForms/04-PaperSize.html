Paper Size

FreeBooks ships its reports and forms drawn for US Letter. Installations outside North America generally print on A4, which is 6mm narrower and 18mm taller, so the paper a company uses is chosen once and applied to every report and form that gets installed.

Choosing the paper

The paper size is asked for in two places, both of them at the moment reports are about to be installed:

  • During installation — under Printing Information on the main install form, alongside the fiscal year settings.
  • When creating a company — on the Company Manager tab, with the current company's setting offered as the default.

Whichever is chosen becomes the company's default paper size, visible afterwards under the General tab of the Reports and Forms settings. Every report and form copied in during that install is stamped onto it, and any report drawn later in the designer starts on it too.

Only the paper size is applied. Orientation is left exactly as each report declares it, because most of the shipped reports are landscape by design and turning them portrait would squeeze their columns off the page.

Changing it afterwards

Changing the default under the General tab affects reports created from that point on; it does not reach back into the reports already installed. To move those, use Re-stamp Paper Size on the Tools tab, which rewrites the page size of every stored report and form to the current default in one pass.

Re-stamping changes the paper and nothing else. It does not move anything drawn on the page, so a form laid out for a wider sheet will sit closer to the right edge on a narrower one; if that matters, the affected elements need nudging in the designer. Any file whose page setting cannot be located is left untouched and counted in the message shown afterwards, so nothing is silently rewritten or lost.

When a report is wider than its paper

A report whose columns add up to more than the printable width of its page is turned landscape automatically at print time, and if it is too wide even then, it is scaled down to fit. Nothing is clipped. If a report prints noticeably small, its columns are wider than the paper can hold and the fix is to narrow a column in the designer, or to move that report to a larger sheet.

Pre-printed stock

Cheques and deposit slips are positioned to match pre-printed bank stationery. Their element positions were drawn for US stock, so changing the paper size will not make them line up with stationery from another country — those forms need their positions adjusted in the designer to match whatever stock is actually being used.
