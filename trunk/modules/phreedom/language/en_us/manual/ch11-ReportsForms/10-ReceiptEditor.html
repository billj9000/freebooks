Changing a Receipt's Layout

A till receipt is not laid out like an invoice. An invoice sits on a page and every item on it has a position; a receipt has no page and no positions at all. Its fields simply print in order, each given a width in characters, and that is the whole of its layout.

So a receipt has an editor of its own, and it looks like what it produces: a list of lines, and in each line the fields written into it.

Getting to it

Open Tools → Form Layout. Receipts are marked in that list, and opening one lands on this editor rather than on the layout canvas — the list knows which kind of document each one is, so you do not have to. Changing a receipt needs the same permission as changing any other form.

The one thing worth knowing first

Fields on a receipt overlay each other. They do not sit side by side.

Each line starts as a row of blank character cells as wide as the paper, and every field on that line is written over what is there already — pushed to the left, to the right, or centred. That is how a label and an amount end up on one line: Subtotal written to the left and its value written to the right are two fields writing into the same forty cells, not two boxes placed at two points.

Tick Ends line on the last field of a line. That is what sends the line to the printer and starts a fresh one.

This behaviour is hard to picture and easy to see, which is why the preview is beside you the whole time.

The preview

The panel on the right shows a real sale printed through the receipt engine itself — not an impression of one. It refreshes as you change things, before you save, so you never have to commit to a change to find out what it does.

Above it is a character ruler. Use it: a cell width of 15 means nothing on its own, only against the column it lands in.

If the company has no point-of-sale entries there is nothing to print, so the preview says so and the lines can still be edited.

This receipt

  • Name — what the receipt is called in the form list.
  • Paper width — how many characters fit across the paper. It is what a new field is given and what the ruler measures. It does not cut anything short: a line longer than this is still printed, and the printer decides what becomes of it.

The lines

Each row of the table is one field. Reading across:

  • Line — the line number, shown once against the first field of each line. Fields sharing a line are the ones with no number of their own.
  • Prints — what the field puts on the paper. Wording you typed can be edited here in place. A field that prints a value instead shows what it is bound to, and cannot be retyped: its wording comes from the sale.
  • Format — how a value is printed: as money, as a date, and so on. It applies only to fields that print a value; wording you typed shows a dash. This matters more on a receipt than elsewhere, because a field occupies a fixed number of character cells — an amount printed as 12.5 where 12.50 belongs is a column that stops lining up.
  • Cell — how many characters wide the field is.
  • Position — Left, Centred or Right, meaning where within its cells the text is placed.
  • Ends line — tick on the last field of each line.
  • Move — the arrows move a field earlier or later, and × removes it. Order is layout here, so moving a field is how you re-arrange a receipt.

The item list is a field like any other, but it prints whole lines of its own, so it always ends its line and the tick is fixed.

Adding to a receipt

Two kinds of thing can be added, and both arrive at the end of the receipt, ending their own line. The arrows then move the new field to where it belongs.

Add a line of text adds wording you type yourself — a thank-you, a returns policy, a line of dashes.

or a value adds a field that prints something from the sale: a total, a date, the customer. The list beside the button holds every value this receipt can supply and nothing else, so anything you can choose is something that will print. Pick a format at the same time — an amount wants a money format — and change it afterwards in the Format column if it is not right.

You cannot type a name of your own. A name that merely looks plausible would save perfectly well and then print nothing, leaving a field that takes up its cells and is blank on every receipt from then on.

If you delete something by accident

Before you save, nothing has happened: × only takes the row off the screen. Press Cancel, or reload the page, and the receipt is exactly as it was.

After a save, add it back — text with Add a line of text, a value with the list beside it — then set its Format, Cell and Position to match, and move it into place. The preview shows you when it is right.

Saving

Nothing is written until you press Save; the preview costs nothing and changes nothing. Cancel returns to the form list and leaves the receipt as it was.

Receipts are printed from the point-of-sale screen in the ordinary way, through the Print button on the sale. See The Print Buttons.
