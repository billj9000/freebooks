Accounting Tutorial

The accounting equation

Every business's finances balance around one equation:

Assets = Liabilities + Equity

Assets are what the company owns (cash, inventory, money owed by customers). Liabilities are what it owes (loans, money owed to vendors). Equity is what's left over — the owners' stake. Every transaction changes this equation, but it must always stay in balance.

Double-entry bookkeeping

FreeBooks, like all real accounting systems, uses double-entry bookkeeping: every transaction is recorded as at least two entries — a debit to one account and a matching credit to another, for the same amount. Debits and credits aren't "good" or "bad"; what they increase or decrease depends on the account type:

  • Assets and Expenses increase with a debit, decrease with a credit.
  • Liabilities, Equity, and Revenue increase with a credit, decrease with a debit.

Because every transaction touches at least two accounts equally, total debits across the company always equal total credits — this is the trial balance, and FreeBooks checks it automatically before a transaction is allowed to post.

Chart of accounts

The Chart of Accounts is the full list of accounts a company posts to, each tagged with a type (asset, liability, equity, revenue, or expense). Every journal entry posts to two or more of these accounts.

A worked example

Say a customer is invoiced $100 for a service. FreeBooks posts:

  • Debit Accounts Receivable $100 (an asset — the customer now owes more)
  • Credit Revenue $100 (revenue increases)

When the customer later pays that invoice, FreeBooks posts:

  • Debit Cash $100 (an asset — the company now has more cash)
  • Credit Accounts Receivable $100 (the customer owes less)

In both cases the debits and credits are equal, and the books stay in balance. Every journal entry in FreeBooks, whether entered directly or generated automatically from a sales order, invoice, or bill, follows this same pattern.

Fiscal periods

Transactions post into a specific accounting period (typically a month), part of the company's fiscal year. See General Ledger Utilities for advancing the current period.
