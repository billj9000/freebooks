Inventory - Product Bundles

A Product Bundle is an inventory item made up of one or more other inventory items and sold as a single line, without ever being built or receiving a stock adjustment of its own. Unlike an Item Assembly, there is no separate assembly step: the quantity available for a Product Bundle is calculated on the fly from its components' stock on hand, so it always reflects what could currently be put together. A Product Bundle's Bill of Materials can always be edited, since it is never locked by posting or by holding stock of its own.

Adding a Product Bundle

  1. From the main menu, select Inventory->Add Item.
  2. Enter a SKU and select "Product Bundle" as the inventory type.
  3. Fill in the item's description and other general fields as with any other inventory item.
  4. On the Bill of Materials tab, add one row per component SKU and the quantity of that component used per bundle.
  5. Press Save. The bundle's quantity on hand will show the number of complete bundles that could currently be assembled from stock on hand.

Master Product Bundles - bundles that vary by attribute

Some bundles need to be sold in more than one variation of otherwise identical contents - for example, a kit sold in red or blue, where only the colour of one component changes. A Master Product Bundle works the same way a Master Stock Item does for plain stock items: it holds the attribute definitions and a template Bill of Materials, and FreeBooks generates one Product Bundle Part per attribute combination automatically. Each generated part is a normal Product Bundle in every other respect - it has its own quantity on hand, calculated the same way, and its own copy of the Bill of Materials - but it can only be created, renamed or deleted through its Master Product Bundle, not on its own.

Adding a Master Product Bundle

  1. From the main menu, select Inventory->Add Item.
  2. Enter a SKU and select "Master Product Bundle" as the inventory type.
  3. On the Master Stock Attributes tab, define one attribute (for example, Colour) and its possible values - or leave this tab empty for now and let it be filled in for you, see below.
  4. On the Bill of Materials tab, enter the template recipe that every variation will start from.
  5. Press Save. FreeBooks creates one Product Bundle Part per attribute combination, appending the attribute codes to the master's SKU (for example, KIT001-RD), and copies the template Bill of Materials into each one.

Linking a component that varies the same way the bundle does

If one of the bundle's components is itself a Master Stock Item with its own colour variants (for example, a hose master with a separate stocked item per colour), name that master directly on the Bill of Materials tab rather than one of its individual colour SKUs. FreeBooks resolves it to the matching variant for each generated Product Bundle Part automatically - the Red bundle gets the master's Red component, the Blue bundle its Blue component, and so on - provided the two use the same attribute codes (both using "RD" for red, for example).

The first time such a master is picked into the Bill of Materials of a bundle that has no attributes of its own yet, FreeBooks offers to fill in the bundle's attribute list from the master's own - so the same colours don't need to be typed in twice. This is a starting point, not a lock: delete any colour the bundle shouldn't offer before saving, the same way any other attribute value is removed.

This automatic matching only works for a bundle with a single attribute (for example, Colour alone, not Colour and Size together) - a two-attribute bundle keeps the plain behaviour described below for every component, linked or not. If a required variant of a linked master doesn't exist yet (for example, the bundle offers a Green option but the linked master has no Green stock item), saving is refused with a message naming exactly what's missing, and nothing is generated or changed until it's fixed.

Note: a Bill of Materials line that is not a linked Master Stock Item - an ordinary stock item, for example - is still copied identically into every generated Product Bundle Part, exactly as entered on the master. If such a component needs to differ for one variation, open that Product Bundle Part directly and edit its own Bill of Materials tab; re-saving the Master Product Bundle afterwards copies its template Bill of Materials back into every part again (recalculating any linked components as it does), overwriting any such per-variation edit - so make changes like that last, after the master's own recipe is finalized. Because re-saving carries this risk, the Save button stays disabled until something on the page has actually changed, and saving a master that already has generated parts asks for confirmation first, naming how many parts will have their Bill of Materials regenerated.
