Inventory - Price Sheets

A price sheet is a reusable formula for calculating an item's price from up to five quantity-break levels. Customer Price Sheets are used for sales pricing; Vendor Price Sheets are used for purchase pricing. The same price sheet applies across every item that uses it, so a price change only has to be made once to affect every item and every order that references it.

Creating a Price Sheet

  1. From the main menu, select Customers->Customer Price Sheets (for sales pricing) or Vendors->Vendor Price Sheets (for purchase pricing).
  2. Press New.
  3. Enter a Price Sheet Name and an Effective Date.
  4. Check Use as Default to make this sheet the fallback used for any item and customer/vendor that doesn't have a more specific price sheet assigned (see Assigning a Price Sheet, below). Only one sheet per type (Customer/Vendor) can be the default at a time; checking this box on a new sheet clears the flag from whichever sheet held it previously.
  5. Fill in each of the five price levels (see Price Levels, below).
  6. Press Save.

Price Levels

Each price sheet has five levels, evaluated independently:

  • Quantity — the quantity break this level represents. Level 1's quantity is informational only; it's always the price used below the Level 2 break.
  • Source — where the starting price for this level comes from: Direct Entry (type the price in directly), Item Cost, Full Price (the item's retail price field), or Price Level 1 (this level's price is derived from what Level 1 calculates to, avoiding the need to re-enter it). Level 1 cannot source from itself, so its Source list omits Price Level 1.
  • Adjustment — an optional modification applied to the source price: Decrease/ Increase by Amount, Decrease/Increase by Percent, Mark up by Percent, Margin by Percent (solves for a price that yields the given profit margin), or Tiered Pricing (blends this level's price with the previous level's price and quantity break, giving a smoothed per-unit price across the two thresholds rather than a hard cliff).
  • Adj. Value — the amount or percentage used by the Adjustment.
  • Rounding — optional rounding of the final level price: Next Dollar (round up to a whole unit), Constant Cents (round down to whole units and fix the decimal remainder, e.g. .99), or Next Increment (round up to the next multiple of the Rnd. Value, e.g. the next $0.25).
  • Rnd. Value — the cents or increment value used by Rounding.

NOTE: If multi-currency is enabled, price sheet values are always entered and stored in the company's default currency.

Assigning a Price Sheet

When an order or invoice is priced, FreeBooks looks for a price sheet in this order and uses the first one it finds:

  1. The Default Price Sheet set on the customer's or vendor's account (General tab).
  2. A price sheet assigned to the specific inventory item through the Price Manager (see Special/ Per-Item Pricing, below).
  3. Whichever Customer or Vendor Price Sheet is currently marked Use as Default.

Revising a Price Sheet

Price changes are tracked as dated revisions rather than edited in place, so historical orders keep showing the price that was actually in effect at the time.

  1. From the Customer Price Sheets or Vendor Price Sheets list, locate the sheet's most recent revision (the Revise button only appears on the highest revision of a given sheet name).
  2. Press Revise. A new revision is created with tomorrow's date as its Effective Date by default, and the previous revision's Expiration Date is set accordingly. Any special/per-item pricing recorded against the old revision is copied forward to the new one.
  3. Adjust the Effective Date and price levels as needed, then press Save.

Deleting a Price Sheet

  1. From the Customer Price Sheets or Vendor Price Sheets list, press the Delete icon on the sheet's row.
  2. Confirm the prompt. Deleting a sheet also removes any special/per-item pricing recorded against it. If the deleted sheet was the type's default, a warning is shown so a new default can be chosen.

Special/Per-Item Pricing

An individual inventory item can override the price sheet it uses, and override that sheet's price levels just for that item, without affecting any other item on the sheet.

  1. From Inventory->Inventory Manager, press the Price Manager icon (spreadsheet icon) on the item's row, or press the equivalent icon on the item's edit screen toolbar.
  2. Each active price sheet of the matching type is shown on its own tab.
  3. Leave Use Default Price Sheet Settings checked to keep using the sheet's own levels for this item, or uncheck it and edit the Level/Quantity/Source/Adjustment/Rounding grid to set a price override just for this item.
  4. Press Save. This also sets the item's assigned price sheet to whichever tab was edited (used by the priority order described under Assigning a Price Sheet).

Looking Up a Calculated Price

The Price Manager lookup popup shows the price every active sheet (of the matching type) calculates for a given item, including any special/per-item override, so the correct level price can be copied directly into a cost or price field instead of calculated by hand.

  1. From Inventory->Load Item Pricing (reached from the Load Item Pricing button on the Customer/ Vendor Price Sheets list), press the Price Manager icon on an item's row.
  2. Click a listed price to copy it into the Cost or Full Price field on the calling row.

Bulk-Editing Item Cost and Price

Inventory->Load Item Pricing lists every inventory item with its Lead Time, Minimum Stock Level, Reorder Quantity, Item Cost, and Full Price in an editable grid, so these fields can be updated for many items at once without opening each item individually. Press Save when finished. This page edits the item's own cost/price fields directly; it does not change any price sheet.
